Operating model · Capacity & priorities

Deciding between running the platforms and delivering the roadmap

Challenge
The same people who kept critical services running also had to deliver the roadmap. Priorities collided, and trade-offs turned into escalations.
What I did
I built and scaled a common operating model that brought capacity, operational load, platform ownership and roadmap commitments into one conversation. With the four tech leads, we made explicit what work had to move whenever a new priority came in.

Result: Leaders could show where capacity was going and agree with stakeholders on which commitments needed to change. The organization gained a shared basis for prioritizing and distributing work.

FinOps · Financial impact

~35% lower cloud cost

Challenge
Cloud consumption had grown with the business, and capacity decisions were rarely tied to their cost.
What I did
I led the maturation of FinOps: reservation and commitment optimization, and decisions on consumption and capacity tied to their impact on cloud spend.

Result: Approximately 35% cost reduction within the reported scope.

Reliability · AI applied to operations

25–35% fewer platform-related incidents

Challenge
Incidents repeated across platforms because knowledge lived in people, not in the system.
What I did
I drove AI-assisted incident analysis together with stronger standards, better documentation, proactive remediation and coordination with the teams involved.

Result: Approximately 25–35% fewer platform-related incidents, the result of several measures working together.

Governance · Regulated environment

~50% less effort to support PCI certification

Challenge
Each certification cycle demanded significant manual effort from the platform teams to prepare evidence.
What I did

Result: Approximately 50% less effort spent preparing and delivering certification evidence over two years.

How I lead

Building high-performing teams is the job

Results like these come from teams, not heroes. This is how I build them.

Lead through leaders

I lead teams through their tech leads, with structured 1:1s, preventive feedback and career plans. I coach leaders on sharing the load, owning their platforms and bringing decisions backed by capacity data.

Evidence: four tech leads across three business domains, with low turnover and continuity in a critical-services environment. I also built the open-source Growth Assessment Framework to give growth conversations a structure.

Make hard decisions explicit

Running the platforms and changing them compete for the same people. I put capacity, risk and commitments in one view, so priority conflicts become explicit decisions with Product and the business instead of escalations.

Evidence: one operating model across multiple platform teams and three business domains.

Lead people through pressure

In a crisis, psychological safety is part of how a team delivers. During the pandemic I put workload balance, burnout prevention and continuity of critical services at the center of how the team worked.

Evidence: in that same period, as Tech Lead DevOps, I moved the team to continuous delivery, and those practices became the foundation for later platform engineering.

Know when to step in

Experience taught me when to decide, when to delegate and when to seek another perspective. I get close to architecture, code or incidents when my involvement helps solve the problem, and step back when it doesn’t.

Evidence: at Koywe my mandate combines the technical roadmap with Product, mentoring and hands-on contributions to critical core-payments components.

Point of view · AI in engineering

AI agents can write the code. Leaders still own the decision.

Organizations are adopting AI coding tools faster than they adapt how work gets reviewed and approved. I’m designing a delivery flow that connects product requirements, architecture decisions, controls and evaluation criteria. An AI-generated change can be sent back with the missing evidence identified and returned to human review. It rests on three rules:

  1. Traceability: every change points back to a requirement and an architectural decision.
  2. Evidence: tests must cover what should happen and what must not.
  3. Accountability: agents propose; people approve, with autonomy granted level by level.
See an illustrative review
Requirement
In a fictional reporting tool, finance users can export reports. Other users have read-only access.
Architecture decision
The export endpoint enforces access on the server. The agent can propose an implementation and tests for review.
Evidence
The submitted tests show that a finance user can export a report. The test that checks whether a read-only user is blocked is missing.
Review decision
Return the change with a request for the missing test. A human reviewer approves once there is evidence for both paths.
